Build a commodity trading risk layer on Microsoft Fabric

Position, mark-to-market, six-bucket P&L attribution, counterparty credit exposure and IFRS 9 hedge effectiveness - computed in T-SQL, read through Direct Lake, and verified against numbers computed somewhere else. Eight steps, roughly 15 hours, no prior Fabric experience assumed.

Why anyone builds this

A commodity trading desk closes its books every day, not every month. By the next morning someone has to answer: what do we hold, what is it worth, why did that change, who owes us more than we allowed, and are our hedges still working? Most desks answer it out of a CTRM system's canned reports plus a spreadsheet, and the spreadsheet is where the answers actually live.

This build puts those five answers on one estate, recomputed from source every day, with a residual bucket that makes the part nobody can explain visible instead of absorbed.

What you are building

LayerWhat it is
Warehouseref / trd / mkt / ops schemas, 120 business days of synthetic CTRM data
Five T-SQL enginescurve pricing, EOD mark-to-market, six-bucket attribution, credit exposure, IFRS 9 hedge tests
Control totals39 frozen figures, reconciled against a second independent implementation
Direct Lake model12 tables, 108 columns, 13 relationships, 82 measures, 3 calculation groups - no import, no refresh
Report4 pages, every value checked against a figure computed in T-SQL first
All data is synthetic, generated from a fixed seed. The fictional client is Serantau Palm Trading Sdn. Bhd. - a Malaysian palm oil trader hedging physical contracts with Bursa Malaysia FCPO futures. No real trades, counterparties or prices appear anywhere.

The build

1

Set up the workspace and read the trial banner

about 20 min

Everything lives in one Fabric workspace on a capacity. Before you build anything, find out how long you have.

  1. Sign in at app.fabric.microsoft.com with a work or school account and start or confirm your Fabric trial. A personal Microsoft account cannot activate one.
  2. Workspaces → New workspace. Under Advanced, set the licence mode to your capacity.
  3. Read the trial expiry banner and write the date into your plan. The API returns no expiry field; the banner is the only source of truth.
Everything you build here dies with the capacity. This project's trial had about 27 days. That is not a footnote - it decides what you can leave until later. Anything needing the live estate (exports, screenshots, a published report someone else will open) has to happen while it exists, and the deadline belongs in the plan on day one, not in the retrospective.
2

Generate the CTRM dataset - and fix the seed

about 1 h

A trading desk's data is the hard part of a CTRM demo: positions must net, hedges must relate to the things they hedge, and the P&L has to actually decompose. The generator in notebooks/generate.py produces 120 business days of it.

  • Reference - calendar, products, counterparties, credit limits, collateral, ECL rates.
  • Trade - physical contracts and their versions, FCPO futures trades, hedge relationships.
  • Market - forward curves and USDMYR, with a deliberate curve restatement.
  • Operations - actualisation, invoices, payments.
cd notebooks
python generate.py          # writes out/data/*.csv and out/sql_batches/*.sql
The calendar is 120 business days, and they are not contiguous. That single fact decides how every time calculation in this build is written - see step 6. Decide it here, deliberately, because changing it later means rewriting the calculation groups.
The seed is the only thing that makes any of this reproducible. This build used 20260906. Every control total, every figure in the report, and every number in the findings is tied to it. Change it and you get a different, equally valid desk - but none of the published numbers will match yours any more.
3

Load the Warehouse

about 45 min

A Fabric Warehouse, not a Lakehouse. It matters later, in a way that is not obvious now - see step 6.

  1. Create a Warehouse in the workspace.
  2. Run sql/000_ddl_sources.sql - the ref, trd, mkt and ops schemas and their tables.
  3. Run sql/source_batches/*.sql in numeric order. They are numbered because the foreign keys need them to be.
The batches are split for a reason, and the split is not cosmetic
Each file is sized to execute inside a single statement timeout. Concatenating them into one big script to save round trips is the obvious optimisation and it fails on the largest tables - which are also the ones you least want to reload.
4

Build the five T-SQL engines

about 3-4 h

Everything the report shows is computed here, in the Warehouse, by stored procedures - not in DAX. The model reads results; it does not derive them.

  1. 01_eng_tables.sql, 01b_hedge_work_tables.sql - the eng schema output tables.
  2. 02_usp_price_requests.sql - curve interpolation to each contract's tenor.
  3. 03_usp_run_eod.sql - the day's mark-to-market and the six-bucket P&L attribution: new trades, price, FX, time, amendments, unexplained.
  4. 04_usp_hedge_tests.sql - IFRS 9 effectiveness per designated relationship.
  5. 05_usp_run_range_and_controls.sql - the driver: run a date range, compute control totals.
  6. 06_usp_freeze_controls.sql - freeze those totals so a later run can be compared against them.
EXEC eng.usp_run_range_and_controls @from_date = '2026-03-16', @to_date = '2026-08-28';
EXEC eng.usp_freeze_controls;
Why the unexplained bucket exists. A P&L attribution that always sums perfectly is either trivial or lying. The sixth bucket is the residual, and keeping it small and visible is the actual engineering. A desk that cannot see its unexplained P&L cannot govern it.
5

Prove it twice, then prove the replay

about 2 h

The engines are checked by a second, independent implementation in pandas. Not a test of the same code - a different implementation of the same specification.

cd engines
python replay.py            # recompute from the source CSVs
python reconcile.py         # compare against control_totals.json
This build reconciled 39 of 39 control totals, and the replay was then proven deterministic: re-running it left all 39 identical in value, with only the frozen_at timestamps moving.
The right signature for a determinism test is: the numbers do not move, the timestamps do. If nothing at all changes you have probably not re-run anything; if a number moves you do not have a replay, you have a second opinion.
Check the tables the replay does NOT rewrite
One attribution table here is materialised by a separate CTAS that the replay never touches. It would have sailed through a comparison that only looked at what the driver rewrites. Checked separately, it reconciled on 120 of 120 days with a worst absolute difference of 0.000000 - but that check was very nearly skipped, and skipping it is how a stale table survives a green run.
6

The Direct Lake model - and the one line that decides everything

about 3 h

Twelve tables read the Warehouse through Direct Lake: no import, no refresh. The model is generated from model/build_tmdl.py, not hand-written.

Read this before you write the connector. A Fabric Warehouse binds through Direct Lake on SQL:
Sql.Database("<sql endpoint>", "<item id>")
A Lakehouse binds through Direct Lake on OneLake: AzureStorage.DataLake(...). They are not interchangeable, and using the wrong one is not a syntax error.
The wrong connector is a LEGAL datasource kind that never binds a table
The OneLake form on a Warehouse validates on create, reads back byte-intact from the service, passes every schema validator in the toolchain, and draws all twelve tables with their thirteen relationships in the modelling canvas. Then every visual returns Missing_References. It cost most of two sessions. Worse: the shape had been copied from a delivered, working, validated sibling project - whose source was a Lakehouse. A second prior project, also Lakehouse, made it look more settled still. PROVE THE CONNECTOR WITH A TWO-TABLE PROBE BEFORE BUILDING ON IT.
cd model
# put YOUR warehouse's SQL endpoint in build_tmdl.py and expressions.tmdl first
python build_tmdl.py

Three calculation groups sit on top: attribution bucket, time intelligence, and currency. Precedences are set explicitly (10 / 20 / 30).

Do not use DAX time intelligence here. The calendar is 120 non-contiguous business days. DATEADD, SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR and friends assume a contiguous date axis and will quietly return the wrong day. Every time calculation in this build is written against an integer day_index instead.
A calculation item can be structurally perfect and meaningless at the grand total
This build's first 'day over day' item differenced the whole filter context against a single prior day and rendered a plausible 15,581,829. The TMDL was correct, the linter passed, and the number was nonsense. It was caught only because the expected value had been computed in T-SQL BEFORE the render was read. State the expected value at the grand total, not just at the leaf.
7

The report - and how to check it is not lying to you

about 3 h

Four pages, generated as PBIR from report/build_report.py: position and MTM, P&L attribution, credit exposure, hedge effectiveness.

cd report
python build_report.py
A clean render is not evidence. A report bound to a broken model and one bound to a working model look identical - same layout, same formatting, same absence of errors. The only thing that distinguishes them is whether the numbers match something computed somewhere else.
So read every value against a figure you computed first. Write the T-SQL, get the number, then open the report. Not the other way round - once you have seen a plausible number on a page it is very hard to disbelieve it.
Read rendered values from the DOM, not from screenshots
Power BI matrix and card visuals render into the DOM. One scripted read returns every value exactly, title attributes included, which is both faster and more reliable than zooming a screenshot until the digits resolve. Reserve screenshots for when the layout itself is the question.
8

Publish - and the two packages that look the same

about 1 h

A .pbip package comes in two bindings, and only one of them can ship a model change.

BindingContainsPublishing it pushes
byConnection<name>.Report onlythe report only - it binds the live model by connection
byPath<name>.Report + <name>.SemanticModelthe report and the model
Both open in Desktop, both publish without complaint, and only one carries a model
This build published a model change from the byConnection package. It succeeded. It pushed the report, changed no model, warned about nothing, and left the workspace reconciling perfectly - because that is what a byConnection package correctly does. Identify byPath by the .SemanticModel folder INSIDE it, never by the directory name.
Working instruction: open the byPath .pbip, Publish, accept the overwrite prompt, and do not save. Desktop rewrites the report's pinned schema versions on save, not on publish - so going straight from open to Publish leaves them intact. If Desktop does not offer the overwrite prompt, stop: landing as a new dataset breaks the item list.
An accessibility binding to a missing measure is dropped SILENTLY
This build's 32 visuals bind their alt text to measures. When those measures were not in the live model, the report rendered 28 containers, zero errors, every figure correct - pixel-identical to a correct build. Nothing anywhere said the accessibility layer was absent. The fix is to build an instrument that CAN fail: bind the alt-text measures as VISIBLE values on a check page, so the visual errors and names them when they are missing. Leave it in place as a regression test.

What this build does not do

Stated plainly, because a portfolio piece that hides its gaps is worth less than one that names them.

  • The open mirrored database is dropped. The landing zone was never populated. The notebook that writes it is sound and is included; it was never run.
  • The Activator alert rules are documented, not live. No Activator tool was reachable in the build sessions. The rules are specified; nothing is firing.
  • No row-level security, no incremental refresh, no CI/CD pipeline. Out of scope.
  • Bursa Malaysia contract specifications are tagged VERIFIED or UNVERIFIED in the project notes. Delivery points and last trading day were not verified and are not used by the generator.
  • One number is left open honestly: a currency calculation-group cell renders -38,079 against a computed -38,079.715775. Both inputs were verified; the cause of the last digit was not established, and it is recorded as open rather than rounded away.
